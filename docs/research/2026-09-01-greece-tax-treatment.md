# Greek tax treatment: UCITS ETFs, listed shares, crypto

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Jurisdiction:** Greece (individual tax resident)
**Status:** sections 1–2 `verified`; section 3 `unverified`
**Supersedes:** an earlier claim in the same session that distributing UCITS ETFs
cost 5% Greek dividend tax and that accumulating funds bought a decade of
deferral. Both were wrong — see section 1.

---

## 1. EU/EEA UCITS funds — capital gains and distributions both exempt

For a Greek tax resident individual, EU/EEA-domiciled UCITS funds are exempt
from Greek income tax on **both** capital gains and dividends. The levy is
applied at fund level and that exhausts the unit-holder's liability
(Law 4172/2013).

| Event | Greek tax |
|---|---|
| Selling an Irish UCITS ETF at a profit | none |
| Distributions from a distributing Irish UCITS ETF | none, no withholding |
| A non-UCITS / US-domiciled ETF (SPY, VOO, VT) | 15% on gains, 5% on dividends |

Consequences for this portfolio:

- The **accumulating vs distributing** choice is **tax-irrelevant** inside the
  ETF sleeve. There is nothing to defer because there is nothing to pay.
- Substituting Irish UCITS equivalents for the plan's original US-domiciled
  picks was not only an access fix (see the instrument-availability entry) — it
  made the sleeve tax-free.

**Exempt is not the same as undeclared.** Exempt amounts still have to be
reported in the correct E1 codes.

## 2. Listed single shares — gains exempt, dividends leak

- **Capital gains on listed shares:** exempt where the holding is under 0.5% of
  the company. Not a constraint at any realistic personal scale.
- **Dividends:** the UCITS exemption does not apply. For US shares: 15% US
  withholding at source (treaty rate, requires a W-8BEN, handled by the broker),
  then 5% Greek dividend tax with credit for the US portion. Since 15% > 5%,
  there is no additional Greek bill — but the 15% US withholding is permanently
  lost.

Net effect: the only recurring tax drag in the portfolio is US withholding on
the dividend-paying single stocks. At ~3% yields on a small position weight this
is a low-single-digit euro amount per year. The sub-1% yielders are negligible.

**Revised conclusion:** the tax case for winding down the dividend-defensive
single stocks was largely wrong, and what survives is trivially small. Any case
against them now rests on portfolio fit (income assets during a growth-oriented
accumulation phase), not on tax.

## 3. Crypto — a 15% regime is described, enactment not confirmed

Consistent across secondary sources:

- 15% flat on realised gains
- first €500 per year exempt
- losses carry forward 5 years
- staking and mining taxed as ordinary income instead (9–44% progressive)
- **DAC8 reporting from 1 July 2026** — the broker reports crypto activity to
  AADE automatically regardless of the domestic rate

**Not verified:** whether the regime is actually in force. Commercial tax-software
guides present it as live 2026 law, while news reporting as recent as June 2026
describes a Finance Ministry bill "expected to be submitted to parliament in the
coming months." No record of a parliamentary vote was found. These cannot both
be correct.

**Practical action regardless of status:** keep acquisition records — date,
quantity, EUR cost — for every crypto purchase. Under a 15%/€500-allowance regime
the cost basis determines the bill, and DAC8 means the authority has the disposal
side whether or not the taxpayer has the purchase side.

**Two precise questions for an accountant:**
1. Is the 15% digital-asset regime in force, and for which tax year?
2. Does the €500 allowance apply per year against total net gains?
